## 2. The data problem, stated plainly

Your timetable produces **7 lecture slots per week**. Between 20 July and 14 August there were **20 working days**, which is exactly **4 full weeks** — so the timetable permits at most **28 lectures**. The app reports **33 held**.

| **Period** | **Working days** | **Timetable max** | **App reports** | **Gap** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 20 – 31 Jul | 10 | 14 | 15 | +1 |
| 3 – 14 Aug | 10 | 14 | 18 | +4 |
| **Total** | **20** | **28** | **33** | **+5** |

**Observed intensity: 1.65 lectures per working day. Timetable intensity: 1.40.** The most likely explanations, in order of plausibility:

1. **Long slots are logged as two sessions.** Design Studio runs 7:30–12:00 (4.5 h) on Tuesday and 12:00–4:30 on Thursday. If those are marked twice, the weekly load is 9, not 7.
2. **Extra or make-up sessions** were held that are not on the handwritten timetable.
3. **Orientation, workshops or guest sessions** in the opening fortnight were logged as attendance.

<aside>
🔑

**This is your highest-value action.** Open the Atlas Tech app, go into the subject-wise breakdown for Sem 5, and count how many sessions each subject shows. If Design Studio shows roughly double the others, explanation 1 is confirmed and your real budget is at the top of the range, not the bottom. Ten minutes of checking is worth more than everything else on this page.

</aside>
